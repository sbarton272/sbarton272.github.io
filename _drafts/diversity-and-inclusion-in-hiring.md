There are two parts to building a culture of diversity and inclusion: hiring with diversity and ensuring those employees can contribute their fullest. When I talk about measuring diversity, I'm referencing under-represented minorities (URM) and non-male folks. URM in tech does not include Asian or White men. The opinions I present here aren't backed up by any data and reflect best practices I've learned working with HR teams.

#### On hiring

When a candidate first interacts with the company brand it's important to put a best foot forward and signal that diversity is something the company is serious about. It's also important to recognize that many URM folks are self-selecting out before even talking to companies if they think they will have a rough experience.

- If you've got a diversity report that can be included in early sourcing or outreach.
- The job description should be inclusive. This can be as obvious as an inclusion statement. More subtle things are not listing years of experience required or specific tech qualifications, as non-male folks can self-select out if they don't match these qualifications.
- All candidates are looking for a good culture. It helps to be up-front about important benefits in the JD, website and other sites like [Key Values](https://www.keyvalues.com/) or Glassdoor. Key benefits for parents to highlight are hours flexibility and paternity leave.

#### Sourcing with diversity in mind

Sourcing candidates is an art to itself, but diversity should be top of mind at this stage.

- Every source has a bias and some bias against URMs. For example AngelList in my experience skews young, white and male.
- Pools like LinkedIn are good because just about everyone is there.
- Specialty groups (ex. Grace Hopper) can be great exposure, albeit expensive for a startup. Connecting with these groups sends an important signal to candidates that you care about diversity.
- Employee referrals are great but you do risk hiring a bunch of folks that are demographically the same. You'll nearly always need to supplement with outreach.
- Outreach targets should be set up front. For example, you might apply a target for URM or non-male outreach to some roles. You'll also want targets for offers since you've got control through the offer stage.

#### Run an inclusive interview

During the interview process you want to demonstrate inclusive values.

- Interviewers should be trained on what's appropriate to talk about (ex. avoid anything personal).
- Interviewers should be trained on unconscious biases.
- If possible the panel can reflect current team diversity. Beware tiring out key team members though.
- Take time to understand what the candidate is interested in knowing about and be transparent.

#### Build an inclusive culture

While it's great to hire with diversity, those new employees also need to stick around. Inclusion is about building a safe working environment so that everyone can contribute their full potential. I don't have a unified philosophy but I do have thoughts.

- Get *everyone* trained: sexual harassment, unconscious bias ([Paradigm](https://www.paradigmiq.com/) is good), inclusive hiring. Ally training was also really helpful for me.
- Push for inclusive processes. Put gender pronouns in Slack and demonstrate sharing them in introductions. Have agendas for all meetings, have note takers and push for more written communication. Non-inclusive meetings can be a problem, so provide more means for contribution.
- Support easy employee resource group (ERG) creation. Provide budget and time for groups to meet and put on events.
- Executives should lead the charge and set an example: diversity targets in company hiring goals, showing up for ERG events, and showing no tolerance for harassment.
- Prioritize written communication. This can be especially helpful for English-as-a-second-language folks.
- Be open with diversity data. Publish a report annually.
- Gather data on inclusion and workplace safety in frequent surveys. Don't rely on management to understand culture, as management can sometimes be the problem. Do provide management aggregated data to take action on.
